About the College

The College of Dentistry is one of the founding colleges of Ain Al Khaleej University of Health Sciences, a private university in Al Ain, Abu Dhabi. The College is part of the Ain Al Khaleej Academic Health System, which brings together the University, Ain Al Khaleej Hospital and the Hospital's outpatient clinics, so that teaching, patient care and research take place in one real, AI-based learning ecosystem.

The College offers the Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BDS) programme. It is a competency-based curriculum built on the five professional competence domains set by the Commission for Academic Accreditation for UAE dental graduates, with a sixth domain the University has added: digital practice and AI-enabled dentistry. The curriculum is benchmarked against international dental education frameworks.

University visionTo lead health sciences education through an integrated clinical excellence and intelligent practice rooted in national values.
University missionTo develop competent healthcare professionals who demonstrate excellent patient care and research through AI-enabled practices, aligned with national values and meeting global standards.

Dean's message

Dean's portrait, 4:5, plain background

Professor Walid ElsayedVCA and Dean - College of Dentistry

We are building this College at a moment when dentistry itself is changing.

Impressions are becoming scans. Radiographs are read with the help of algorithms. Restorations are designed on a screen and milled beside the chair. A new college has one real advantage here: it can be designed for that world from the first day instead of being adapted to it later.

That is what we have tried to do. Every student in our Simulation Centre has their own phantom-head unit and their own operating microscope, because precision is learned early or not at all. Digital dentistry and artificial intelligence are the DNA of our curriculum. They shape every year of study and keep the programme evolving. From the sixth semester our students treat patients under close supervision in the University's Dental Teaching Clinic and at Ain Al Khaleej Hospital.

Technology is not the point, though. The point is a graduate whom a patient can trust: someone who listens well, explains the options honestly, knows the evidence, and knows the limits of their own skill. We judge our students the way the profession will judge them, by what they can be trusted to do, observed many times over five years, and not by a single examination.

My own academic life, with qualifications in both dentistry and health professions education, has taught me that excellence in dentistry begins with curiosity. I hope you will bring yours to Al Ain.

Walid Elsayed

VCA and Dean - College of Dentistry

Student support

We believe that students who are cared for become clinicians who care.

Dentistry is a demanding degree, and the hardest parts are not always academic. Every service below is open to all BDS students from the first day, and conversations with mentors and counsellors are confidential.

A faculty mentor for every student

Each student is assigned a full-time faculty mentor. You meet at least once a semester, after a short self-assessment, to talk through progress, study habits and career plans.

Help when a course goes badly

A student who falls short on any assessment receives an individual learning plan. The University's learning platform flags gaps early, and mentors review each student's progress.

Counselling and wellbeing

The Student Support and Wellbeing Centre offers confidential counselling throughout the academic year, with referral to specialist care where needed.

Students of determination

Reasonable adjustments to teaching, assessment, clinics and accommodation are arranged through the Wellbeing Centre under the University's Students of Determination Policy.

Health and clinical safety

All students hold health insurance and have a health check at registration. Before clinical rotations students complete immunisation and screening, including hepatitis B, BCG and tetanus, and hold third-party liability insurance for clinical training.

Housing and transport

The student residence has single-occupancy studios with 24-hour security and separate provision for female students. Buses run between the residence and campus, and day students can book a seat on set Al Ain routes.

Career guidance

Career Services offers counselling all year, and works with the Internship Office to prepare final-year students for the internship and the licensing examination.

Your voice

Each class elects representatives, and students sit on the College's Curriculum and Assessment Committee. Course evaluations, end-of-phase reviews and a confidential channel for concerns about professionalism or safety feed into College decisions. Formal complaints follow the Student Appeals and Grievances Policy.

Programme

Bachelor of Dental Surgery

The Bachelor of Dental Surgery (BDS) is a five-year, full-time programme of 206 credit hours, delivered on campus and fully aligned with the UAE Professional Competence Standards for Graduates of Baccalaureate Dental Programmes. It follows an integrated, competency-based curriculum built on the five professional competence domains of the UAE Dental Competency Framework, complemented by a sixth AKUHS domain, Digital Practice and AI-Enabled Dentistry. The curriculum is delivered in three progressive phases, from foundational sciences to comprehensive clinical practice. Clinical exposure begins early. Competence is developed step by step and assessed throughout the programme. Preclinical training is delivered in a state-of-the-art dental simulation centre and digital dentistry laboratory, while clinical training takes place at the AKUHS Dental Teaching Clinic and Ain Al Khaleej Hospital, a JCI-accredited healthcare facility. After graduation, a one-year internship leads to UAE licensure as a general dental practitioner.

Facilities

Photo needed: Simulation Centre, wide shot showing phantom heads and microscopes, 16:7
A phantom-head unit and an operating microscope for every student

Dental Simulation Centre

One student to one station. Each unit reproduces chair geometry, lighting, suction and handpieces. Students work under magnification from Year 2, which builds the fine control needed in endodontics, restorative dentistry and periodontal microsurgery. Stations are recorded so students and assessors can review performance, and instructors can broadcast a demonstration to every screen.

Inside our Academic Health System

Dental Teaching Clinic

The dental clinics are located within the Ain Al Khaleej Academic Health System. Students receive their clinical training in the same place where patients are treated every day. They work under the close supervision of well-trained clinical faculty, who guide and approve each step of care. The clinic has intraoral, panoramic, cephalometric and cone-beam CT imaging, chairside scanners, a central sterilisation unit, and suites set up for children and for people of determination.

Scan, design, mill, print

Digital Dentistry Laboratory

Intraoral and desktop scanners, CAD/CAM design stations, milling units and 3D printers, implant-planning software linked to cone-beam CT, digital smile design, and AI-assisted radiograph reading used under teaching protocols. Restorations and appliances that students make here go into supervised patient care.

Conventional and digital workflows side by side

Prosthodontics Laboratory

Individual benches for making complete and partial dentures, fixed restorations and provisionals, with articulators, facebows and zones for wax-up, casting, curing and finishing. The laboratory connects directly to the Digital Dentistry Laboratory.

Dental technicians behind every case

Dental Production Laboratory

A working production laboratory staffed by qualified dental technicians. Using advanced digital and conventional equipment, the team manufactures the crowns, bridges, dentures and appliances that students prescribe for their patients. The laboratory supports and underpins students' clinical work, and shows them how dentist and technician work together.

Shared with the other colleges

Anatomy and Biomedical Sciences Centre, Library, AI Learning Ecosystem

Prosected specimens, plastinated models and virtual dissection tables with dedicated head and neck provision. A library with dental databases including Cochrane Oral Health and ClinicalKey for Dentistry. A University learning platform that adapts study pathways, tutors clinical reasoning, and gives each student and the Assessment Committee a running view of progress.

Research

Research areas

The College's five-year research strategy has four priority themes, chosen to match Abu Dhabi's priorities in life sciences, digital health and AI.

Clinical and dental AI

Developing and validating AI for dental radiology, caries detection, orthodontic planning and clinical decision support.

Digital dentistry and biomaterials

CAD/CAM, intraoral scanning, 3D printing and materials research that carries into general practice.

Oral health of the UAE population

Epidemiology and school-based prevention, translated into oral-health policy.

Health professions education

Scholarship in competency-based education, programmatic assessment, simulation and AI-enabled teaching.

Research opportunities

The University invests in research from within. Faculty can apply for Internal Research Grants, which provide competitive seed funding for new projects. Dedicated funding supports student and graduate research. The Research and Innovation Office helps researchers prepare applications for national and international grants, and guides every project through ethical review.

Industrial collaboration

The College works with industry and academic partners to turn ideas into practice. Collaborative projects focus on digital dentistry, dental materials and AI-enabled clinical tools. Partners can sponsor research through named grants, and joint work is protected by the University's policy on intellectual property and commercialisation.

Student research

Research is part of every student's journey. Training in research methods and biostatistics leads to a final-year research project, completed with a dissertation, a poster and an oral defence. Student work receives ethical review, and projects are presented at the annual Student Research Day.

Careers

After the BDS, graduates complete a one-year supervised internship and then sit the licensing examination of the relevant UAE health authority (the Ministry of Health and Prevention, the Department of Health Abu Dhabi or the Dubai Health Authority) to register as general dental practitioners.

Most graduates go into general practice in government clinics, private practices and group networks. Others continue to specialty training in fields such as orthodontics, endodontics, periodontology and implantology, or prosthodontics, or move into academic, research and public oral health roles.
